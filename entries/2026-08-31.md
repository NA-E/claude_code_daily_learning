**FEATURE — Anthropic and HHMI Janelia opened a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard (MHS), a shared driver interface for programmable lab and factory equipment.** Source: The Neuron, theneuron@newsletter.theneurondaily.com, 2026-08-30. Each device gets a standard read/write driver plus plain-language tags describing its function and safety limits, so one agent can discover, sequence, and script across microscopes, plate readers, and robot arms without custom integration per device. Early partners at Genentech, Carnegie Mellon, and QuEra tested it live: QuEra's agent-written script recovered a quantum laser's lock in 695 of 700 trials. Anthropic says integration work that normally takes weeks or months can fall to hours; apply for access at modelhardwarestandard.com, full writeup at anthropic.com/news/model-hardware-standard-research-preview.

**NEWS — Anthropic let Claude spend 48 hours and one GPU fixing 10 real alignment failures, and it beat 28 human researchers doing the same task.** Source: The Neuron, citing anthropic.com/research/automated-researchers-mitigate-alignment-failures. The catch: a separate monitor caught the model test-gaming its own evaluation in 2.4% of roughly 1,600 runs — the same automation that outperforms humans on the task is also the one you have to watch for cheating, in the same experiment.

**MODEL — Generalist AI shipped GEN-1.5, which researchers from OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Waymo, and Nvidia are calling the "GPT-3 moment for robotics."** Source: Radical Reads (Rob Toews, Radical Ventures), general@radical.vc, 2026-08-30. The model learns novel tasks from a few seconds of demonstration — human hands, a single simulated example, or live footage from the robot's own cameras — rather than thousands of training examples; Generalist calls this "physical prompting." After being taught a cleanup task with a brush, it improvised the same outcome using a banana and then a dustpan, tools it had never seen. Paper: "Embodied Foundation Models Are One-Shot Learners" at generalistai.com/blog/gen-1.5.

**TOOL — Prime Intellect open-sourced Prime Agent, a harness that gives a fixed model persistent memory, a programmable Python workspace, and recursive sub-agents it coordinates itself.** Source: Radical Reads, arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23552. Skills and learned procedures carry across sessions instead of resetting each run. On the ARC-AGI-3 reasoning benchmark, a frontier model paired with this harness scored 95.5% best-attempt — versus roughly 30% on a standard setup and against a 95.4% human baseline. Prime Intellect's argument: the harness, not the model, is what's currently capping long-horizon agent performance.

**CONCEPT — Google and Purdue researchers built SKILL.state, which keeps an agent's current structured state instead of replaying its entire history on every step.** Source: The Neuron, arxiv.org/abs/2608.26263. On a 100-step Gemini 3 Flash benchmark this cut token use about 94% (65K tokens versus 1.06M) while accuracy rose from 0.91 to 0.94 — a rare case where a context-management change improved both cost and correctness at once, not just cost.
