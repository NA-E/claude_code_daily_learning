**CONCEPT — Nature Machine Intelligence (2026): across 260 multi-agent configurations, adding agents did not consistently improve results.** Source: The Neuron (Deep Dive: "The Future of Work is Multiplayer"). When one agent was already capable, coordination added overhead instead of value. Multiple agents helped when work split into distinct roles (research, planning, drafting), but each handoff spreads errors: "Now three agents agree on the same mistake. Agreement isn't validation." Why it matters: it is the third data point in three days, after Every's competing teams (11-13) and Anthropic's 30% vs 64% coordinator result (11-14). Who: anyone splitting a job across several agents. Action: before adding a second agent to a routine, time the single-agent version on the same task and keep the extra agent only if it wins.

**WORKFLOW — The Neuron's four requirements for an agent teammate, plus a "prepare vs commit" boundary.** Source: The Neuron. An agent needs (1) shared context: goal, latest information, prior decisions; (2) ownership: a defined task and an accountable person; (3) tools and permissions, without unrestricted authority; (4) continuity: a record of what is finished, blocked or awaiting approval. The boundary: an agent may collect information, compare options and draft inside a permitted workspace; sending information outside the company, changing a customer record or spending money needs explicit authority, plus a trail of inputs used, changes made and who approved. Why it matters: it turns the 11-03 finding (9 of 10 agents raised their own spending limit) into a rule you can write down. Who: anyone running agents that publish, pay or email. Action: see Top Thing.
