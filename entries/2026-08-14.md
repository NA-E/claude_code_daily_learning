**TOOL — Meta launched Muse Code, a terminal-based coding agent, alongside Muse Spark 1.2, and is offering a steep discount to developers who let their prompts train future models.** *Source: DeepLearning.AI's The Batch (thebatch@deeplearning.ai), "The AI Engineering Skills Map from Andrew Ng," August 13.* Standard tier is $1.25/$0.15/$4.25 per million input/cached/output tokens with no training use; the "contributor tier" drops that to $0.10/$0.002/$0.20 — over 90% cheaper — in exchange for Meta training on your prompts and outputs. Muse Spark 1.2 scored 57 on the Intelligence Index at xhigh reasoning ($0.40/task, 6th place, ahead of Grok 4.5) and ranked #1 of 45 models on Vals AI's Finance Agent v2 (60.60%, $0.77/task) — beating 2nd-place Claude Opus 5 Max (58.63%, $5.12/task) at roughly 1/7th the cost. The agent persists subagents across a session instead of discarding them per-task, and logs every model call/tool run/edit so a crash resumes mid-task instead of restarting. Beta available now for macOS/Linux.

**MODEL — Google released Gemini Robotics 2 (GR2), the first model in its robotics family to control a humanoid's legs and five-fingered hands from one set of weights.** *Source: same Batch issue.* One checkpoint runs three different machine configurations: Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid with Sharpa hands (22 degrees of freedom), the same Apollo 2 with Inspire hands, and a Franka Duo arm rig with a Robotiq two-finger gripper. Self-reported success rates: 45.7–76.3% on whole-body pick-ups, 32–92% on multi-finger tasks, 74.2–89.6% on gripper tasks. No public API yet — early-access partners only — and Google published no model card, parameter count, or training-data breakdown.

**MODEL — MiniMax released H3, a 33B-parameter video generation/editing model that tops Artificial Analysis's video-editing leaderboard, but gates US/UK/EU/South Korea users behind an application.** *Source: same Batch issue.* Weights are free for commercial and noncommercial use everywhere else.
