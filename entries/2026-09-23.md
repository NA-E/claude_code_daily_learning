**FEATURE — Don't manually `/compact` a coding-agent session just to tidy up before a break.** Source: theneuron, "Why Amazon blocked Meta's Muse," same 2026-09-22 issue, AI Skill of the Day, citing Kun Chen (substack.com/@kunchenguid/note/c-321238149) and OpenAI's own Codex loop writeup (openai.com/index/unrolling-the-codex-agent-loop). Manual compaction triggers a full summarization pass over the whole context — expensive on a large session, and the rebuilt context loses prior cache savings. Codex, for example, auto-compacts once its own token limit is exceeded. Rule: let the harness auto-compact at its tuned threshold; only compact manually when context pressure is actively hurting the task.

**CONCEPT — Grok 4.7 posted a specialization result on electrical engineering that argues against picking one "best model" for everything.** Source: theneuron, same issue, Tuesday Tech Dive, citing x.ai/news/grok-4-7. **CONCEPT — A "Consequence × Detectability × Recovery" framework for deciding whether to ship a hallucinating AI feature.** Source: marily@substack.com, "AI PM Flashcard #2: When Is It Safe to Ship Hallucinations?," 2026-09-22, marily.substack.com/p/ai-pm-flashcard-2-when-is-it-safe. Core argument: a bare hallucination rate (e.g. "3%") tells you almost nothing — a 97%-accurate product whose failures are obvious and harmless can be safer to ship than a 99.5%-accurate one whose failures are subtle, confidently stated, and hard to catch. Break the failure rate down by severity and type before setting a launch bar, and design the recovery path (retry, edit, undo, escalate to human) as deliberately as the happy path — because the same underlying error rate carries very different product risk depending on whether the user can catch it and undo it.
