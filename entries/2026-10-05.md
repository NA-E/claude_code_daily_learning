**CONCEPT — Filesystem memory for agents: retrieval matters more than the writer, and reorganizing silently deletes facts.** Source: NLPiation #42 (nlpiation.substack.com/p/nlpiation-42-reasoning-attention), paper "Filesystem-Based Memory for LLM Agents." On PersonaMem 128k the weakest memory writer scored 73.8% and the strongest 71.4%, but a stronger search agent raised average accuracy from about 62% to 79%. The same model built 2 files with normal tools and 147 with a shell. When asked to reorganize memory it silently dropped details: REALTALK accuracy fell from 77.6% to 41.2% until a rule "keep every fact" was added. A weak agent with well-written memory (89.3%) beat a stronger one on raw logs (88.6%). Why it matters: this repo's entries/ folder is exactly this kind of memory. Action: when you ask Claude to consolidate notes, add "keep every fact" to the prompt.

**CONCEPT — Models can reason in hidden filler tokens, and the steps can be decoded.** Source: NLPiation #42, "Reading Between the Dots." On open-weight DeepSeek V3 and Kimi K2, inserting meaningless dots lets the model compute "Mozart's age at death plus the atomic number of iridium": the hidden states recover 35, 77 and the answer 112 via logit lens plus residual subtraction. Why it matters: visible chain of thought may not show all the computation. Action: none; a note for when you read model "reasoning" logs.

**CONCEPT — Capitalizing the key word steers attention, but only uppercase helped.** Source: NLPiation #42, paper "Attention is Case-Sensitive." Across 15 casing patterns, LLaMA-3.1-8B went from 29.59% to 44.39% on MMLU-Pro with the target in uppercase. On Qwen2.5-7B, uppercase raised accuracy 1.85 points while alternating case raised attention 2.77 points but cut accuracy 2.83. Reasoning models were much less sensitive. Action: on a small model, try UPPERCASE on the one constraint it keeps ignoring; do not use alternating case.
