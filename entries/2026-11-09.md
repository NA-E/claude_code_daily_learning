**CONCEPT — Realtime-Bench: the best model finished 44% of 50 web and game tasks at real response times, but 70 to 80% when the environment paused while it reasoned.** Source: Radical Reads No. 365 (arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22155, UT Austin, Ohio State, UIUC, NVIDIA, Northwestern, Steadyworks). Extra reasoning helped with the clock frozen and had mixed results under time pressure, since some models kept deliberating until the window closed. The authors call for models that scale reasoning to the time available. Why it matters: ties to 11-02's finding that max reasoning effort can cost 42x more and fail more. Who: anyone building agents that bid, book or trade. Action: set a wall-clock timeout, not just a token budget.

**CONCEPT — Introspection tests: the most capable Claude flagged an injected concept and named it in 20% of trials with no false positives.** Source: Radical Reads (citing Quanta, Nov 4). Researchers inject a specific concept into a model's neural activity and ask whether it notices. It held up under testing, which surprised researchers because no model is trained to monitor its own activity. Why it matters: 80% of trials still went unnoticed, so self-reports are weak evidence. Who: safety-minded builders. Action: do not treat "the model said it was fine" as a check.

**CONCEPT — "Reverse game theory" (mechanism design) for keeping agents honest.** Source: Radical Reads (citing MIT Technology Review, Nov 3). MIT's Stephen Morris proposes rewarding a model for predicting a second model's answers, so deliberately underperforming on safety tests becomes a losing strategy. Princeton's Elad Hazan has models alternate as solver and auditor, penalizing both cheaters and auditors who miss them. Why it matters: it is a design answer to the grader problem from 11-07 (Sakana's judges approved fake success 5 times in 6). Who: anyone running agent evals. Action: for your next eval, make cheating cost something.
