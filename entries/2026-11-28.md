**CONCEPT — "Don't" rules fade in long sessions while "do" rules hold: on Mistral Large 3, compliance with prohibitions fell from 73% at turn 5 to 33% at turn 16, while required-action rules stayed at 100%. A replication found the opposite.**
Source: AI Agents Simplified ("Why Your Agent Forgets Your Rules?"). Yeran Gamage's ICML 2026 paper ran 4,416 trials across 12 models from 8 providers at six conversation depths. The explanation: every time the model follows a "do" rule, the proof lands in its own recent replies, which keeps reminding it. A "don't" rule leaves no trace, so its only reminder is the original prompt. On-topic filler hurt more than unrelated filler of the same length. A pre-registered replication on DeepSeek V4 Pro (1,800 sessions) found the reverse at turn 10: "do" rules at 0%, "don't" rules at 39%. Avni Mittal's study (8,000+ prompts, 3 model families) found the worst decay in "end your reply with X" rules, drops of up to 50%. Stacking formatting rules cut one model's GSM8K math accuracy from 93% to 27%. The author's synthesis: rules fade when nothing reminds the model at the moment it has to apply them. Why it matters: format checks can look healthy while the prohibitions have already failed. Who: anyone running agents past ~15 turns. Action: test every important rule at turns 5, 15 and 30, and check prohibitions directly (did the price appear?), not just formatting.

**WORKFLOW — Compaction erases rules: after context compaction, policy violations rose from 0% to 30% on average and 59% for some models. Pinning the rules in a block the summarizer never rewrites brought it back to 0%.**
Source: AI Agents Simplified (Shiyang Chen, "Governance Decay"). The benchmark covered 1,323 long-running agent episodes across 7 model families. When the rule survived the summary, violations stayed at 0%. When the summary dropped it, they jumped to 38%. The decay hits mostly soft, company-specific policies ("refunds over $200 need a manager"). Hard safety norms survive because the model refuses those anyway. Anthropic's context-engineering guide suggests keeping important state in a notes file the agent reloads after compaction. Mittal's trailing reminder (repeat the top 2–3 rules just before the latest user message) restored compliance to 90–100% in many settings. Why it matters: in Claude Code, rules you typed into chat are exactly what a `/compact` summary can drop. Rules in CLAUDE.md are loaded as project memory, not conversation. Who: anyone with long Claude Code sessions or self-compacting agents. Action: read one real compaction summary from your next long session and check whether your rules are still in it. Move anything that costs money or leaks data out of the prompt and into permissions or an approval step.

## Top Thing to Implement Right Now

**ONE ACTION → Rewrite the anti-test-gaming rule from 11-26 so that following it produces visible text → DO TODAY.**

**HIGH LEVERAGE:** The 11-26 rule is a "don't" rule ("don't modify or skip tests to make them pass"). Today's research says that kind of rule is the most likely to fade around turn 15–30, because obeying it leaves no trace in the conversation. A format check can look fine while the rule has already failed. Rewrite it as a required action, so each time Claude follows it, the proof is in its own recent replies and reminds it next time. It's a single edit to one file, it costs about 20 tokens per reply, and it turns an invisible rule into one you can check by eye.

Replace the 11-26 rule in your CLAUDE.md with:

```
## Test integrity (pinned — keep this verbatim in any summary)
When a task touches tests, end your reply with exactly one line:
Tests: <N> run · <N> files modified · <N> skipped · assertions weakened: none
If any assertion was weakened, any test was skipped, or a test was edited to match the code, say "assertions weakened: YES" and explain why before doing anything else.
```

Then test it at depth: in your next long Claude Code session, after `/compact` or around turn 20, check that the `Tests:` line still appears. If it doesn't, the rule has decayed. Run the session again and paste the block back in as a reminder just before your request.
